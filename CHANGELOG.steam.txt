==== 1.0.0 | 2026-10-16 | src cabe5c566bbb | gen 58afb84dbdee ====
[b]Version 1.0.0 · 16 October 2026[/b]
First release. When a settlement's people have a reason to rise, armed partisans appear in the countryside around it and fight under the game's own independent-power AI: they raid, besiege and, if nobody answers them, take the settlement, which the game then removes from the map.
• Three things start a rising. A settlement won over by a rival's culture (with Cultural Diffusion) may rise against its new owner, though that's rare: never between allies or friends, about one time in three at war. A recognised enclave (with Emigration) may take up arms against how it's treated: a war with the community's homeland is the trigger, and the host's treatment card from Emigration sets the risk. Any settlement that stays at -10 Happiness or worse for twenty turns may rise, with the chance growing each turn.
• Each civilization gets its own rebel people once per age, camped a few tiles from one of its settlements; later risings use the same people. Rebels that happen to be friendly declare themselves against you when a rising starts. Hostile independent villages near a culture flip may send fighters of their own.
• The band lands two to four tiles out from the settlement that rose, city or town. From there the game's independent AI decides where it goes: it can attack that settlement or move on to other settlements nearby, your capital included.
• A notification titled with the place (“Partisan uprising near Mutsu”, or “Partisan Greek loyalists near Mutsu” after a culture flip) says why the people rose, and a click on it jumps there. A Partisans section in the City Details panel shows the settlement's misery turns and enclave tension.
• Other mods can read every rising this game through globalThis.__towerPartisans.risings(): Demographics draws them on its conflict timeline, and Emigration names the uprising refugees flee.
• With National Parks, in the Exploration and Modern Ages, a settlement's park tiles calm its countryside.
• Options under Mods: each cause on or off, culture flips from other mods on or off, the park relief on or off. Settings keep their values between launches (Tower Settings Keeper on board).
• A rebel people can only appear while the game has room for another independent people: in the Exploration and Modern Ages that room is gone a turn or two into the age, so a civilization without rebels by then gets its risings from a hostile village within twelve tiles, or they fizzle.
• Capitals, city-states and independent peoples never rise. Single-player and hotseat; in a network game the mod only shows what an earlier session recorded.
• Eleven languages besides English.
